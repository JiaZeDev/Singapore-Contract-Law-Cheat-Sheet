# Singapore Contract Law Cheat Sheet

> **Study aid only — not legal advice.** This is a high-level overview of Singapore contract law. Always check the current legislation and case law, and seek qualified legal advice for a real dispute.

## 1. Contract formation

A simple contract is generally formed when the parties reach an enforceable agreement. Check for:

- **Offer:** A clear, definite proposal showing willingness to be bound if accepted. Distinguish an offer from an invitation to treat, such as many advertisements or goods displayed for sale.
- **Acceptance:** An unqualified agreement to the offer, communicated in the manner required or permitted. A counter-offer generally rejects and replaces the original offer. Silence is generally not acceptance.
- **Consideration:** Something of legal value given in exchange for a promise, unless the agreement is made by deed or another rule applies. Consideration need not be economically adequate, but must be legally sufficient. Past consideration is generally insufficient.
- **Intention to create legal relations:** Objectively assessed from the words and circumstances. Commercial arrangements are generally presumed to be intended to have legal effect; social or domestic arrangements generally are not. These are rebuttable presumptions.
- **Certainty and completeness:** The essential terms must be sufficiently certain for a court to determine what the parties agreed.

### Offer and acceptance: quick checks

- Was the proposal communicated and sufficiently definite?
- Had it expired, been revoked, or been rejected before acceptance?
- Did the response accept the offer as made, or introduce a new term?
- For online or written agreements, when and how did acceptance take effect?

## 2. Contract terms

### Express and implied terms

- **Express terms** are agreed in words, whether written or oral.
- **Implied terms** may arise under legislation, established custom, or the circumstances and presumed intentions of the parties. Implication is subject to applicable legal tests and is not automatic.

### Classifying terms and breach

- **Condition:** A breach may entitle the innocent party to terminate and claim damages.
- **Warranty:** A breach generally gives a right to damages, but not termination.
- **Innominate (intermediate) term:** The remedy depends on the consequences of the breach—particularly whether they substantially deprive the innocent party of the contract's benefit.

Classification depends on the contract's construction and applicable law; labels used by the parties are not always decisive.

### Exclusion and limitation clauses

Ask whether the clause was incorporated into the contract, whether it covers the breach or loss in question, and whether legislation or other rules restrict its effect. Courts interpret clauses according to their wording and context. Consumer and other statutory protections may apply.

## 3. Factors affecting validity or consent

- **Misrepresentation:** A false statement of fact or law that induced the other party to enter the contract may give rise to remedies. Identify whether it was fraudulent, negligent, or innocent; the available remedy depends on the facts and applicable law.
- **Mistake:** A sufficiently serious mistake may affect whether a contract was formed or make it void or voidable in limited circumstances. Identify what was mistaken and whether the mistake was shared or unilateral.
- **Duress:** Illegitimate pressure that caused a party to contract may make the contract voidable. Economic duress requires more than commercial pressure alone.
- **Undue influence:** Improper influence may make a transaction voidable. Consider the relationship, the circumstances of the transaction, and whether the other party can rebut any applicable presumption.
- **Unconscionability:** In appropriate circumstances, a court may grant relief where one party has taken unconscientious advantage of another's weakness. The requirements are fact-sensitive.
- **Capacity and authority:** Check whether each party had legal capacity and, for an organisation, whether the person acting had authority.
- **Illegality and public policy:** An illegal agreement or one contrary to public policy may be unenforceable, but the consequences depend on the relevant rule and circumstances.

## 4. Performance, breach, and discharge

### Performance

Determine what each party promised, whether performance was due, and whether it was performed as required. A party's tender of performance may matter if the other party refuses to accept it.

### Breach

A breach occurs when a party fails to perform a contractual obligation. It may be an actual breach or an anticipatory breach, where a party clearly indicates before performance is due that it will not perform.

Termination is not available for every breach. It generally depends on the term breached, the seriousness and consequences of the breach, and any termination clause. The innocent party may also need to elect whether to affirm or terminate the contract.

### Discharge

A contract may be discharged by:

- Performance
- Agreement (including a valid variation, rescission, or novation)
- A contractual termination right or a right arising from repudiatory breach
- **Frustration:** A supervening event, without the fault of the party relying on it, makes performance impossible, unlawful, or radically different from what was undertaken. Mere inconvenience, delay, or increased expense will not usually be enough. The Singapore Frustrated Contracts Act (Cap. 115) may govern the financial consequences of frustration.

## 5. Remedies

### Damages

The usual remedy for breach is damages. The aim is generally to put the claimant, so far as money can, in the position they would have been in had the contract been performed. Consider:

- **Causation:** Did the breach cause the claimed loss?
- **Remoteness:** Was the type of loss within the legally recoverable scope?
- **Mitigation:** Did the claimant take reasonable steps to limit loss?
- **Proof and measure:** Can the loss be established and quantified?

A sum stated in a contract as payable on breach is not automatically enforceable just because it is called “liquidated damages”; the applicable penalty doctrine and the clause's substance must be considered.

### Other remedies

- **Specific performance:** A discretionary order requiring performance; generally considered where damages are inadequate and the legal requirements are met.
- **Injunction:** A discretionary order restraining conduct or, in limited cases, requiring action.
- **Rescission:** Sets aside a voidable contract and seeks to restore the parties, so far as possible, to their pre-contract positions. Bars to rescission may apply.
- **Rectification:** Corrects a written document that fails to record the parties' actual agreement, where the legal requirements are satisfied.
- **Restitution:** May require a benefit to be returned where a recognised legal basis exists.

## 6. Third parties and assignment

### Privity

As a general rule, only a party to a contract can enforce it. Singapore's **Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 2001** provides exceptions: a third party may, in qualifying circumstances, enforce a term that expressly allows it or purports to confer a benefit on it, subject to the Act and the contract's terms.

### Assignment

Contractual benefits may generally be assigned subject to the contract and applicable law. Contractual burdens cannot ordinarily be transferred without the other party's agreement; a novation may be used where all relevant parties agree to substitute a party.

## 7. Problem-question checklist

Use this sequence:

1. **Formation:** Offer, acceptance, consideration, intention, certainty.
2. **Terms:** What was promised? Which terms are express or implied? Any exclusion clause?
3. **Validity:** Misrepresentation, mistake, duress, undue influence, capacity, authority, illegality?
4. **Performance:** What was due, when was it due, and what happened?
5. **Breach and termination:** Was there a breach? Does it justify termination, or is there a contractual right?
6. **Remedy:** Damages or another remedy? Address causation, remoteness, mitigation, and any bars.
7. **Third parties or transfer:** Does privity, the 2001 Act, assignment, or novation matter?

## 8. Quick revision prompts

- Do not confuse an **offer** with an **invitation to treat**.
- A **counter-offer** is generally not acceptance of the original offer.
- Separate **formation**, **validity**, **breach**, **termination**, and **remedy**.
- Not every breach permits termination.
- Frustration is narrow; increased cost or difficulty alone is usually insufficient.
- A contractual label does not determine whether a term is a condition, warranty, or enforceable damages clause.
- Apply the legal test to the facts, and support conclusions with relevant Singapore authorities in assessed work.
